Selected work / Social media analysis

Subway UAE: the Meatball Marinara comeback

A critical analysis of a real campaign: what it was built to do, how its content mix worked, and where it undercut the brand's "fresh" promise.

Academic project. Group of three, MKT 306 Social Media Advertising, Canadian University Dubai, 2025-26. I did not work for Subway and the company did not commission or use this analysis. No campaign performance data was available, so none is claimed.

What I personally contributed

  • Objective inference and context fit
  • The social media mix audit

The campaign

In April 2026 Subway UAE brought back a sandwich fans had been asking for. The campaign ran on Instagram and TikTok in three stages.

Tease

10 to 15 April

Nostalgia teasers, a "Guess who?" post and a riddle giveaway.

Reveal

15 April

A cinematic reveal built on close-ups of melted cheese and sauce.

Amplify

22 to 26 April

Fan-message video, then influencer reactions.

My analysis: what was the campaign for?

What I looked at

Whether the campaign was a launch, an awareness push or something else.

What I found

It was demand-driven. The sandwich already had fans, so the campaign positioned Subway as granting a request, and prizes pushed people to act, not just watch.

Why it matters

The tease, reveal, amplify structure suits fast-scrolling feeds. Influencer posts landed before the brand's own reveal, which adds reach but blurs where promotion ends.

My analysis: did the content mix hold together?

The mix leans on short video, with studio-quality food shots, fake chat and notification interfaces, and handheld influencer clips. It blends into the feed well. I found three contradictions.

Aesthetic vs realityPolished studio shots next to handheld influencer clips
The jump in production quality makes the "perfect" studio sandwich harder to believe, and a real sandwich will not match it.
Gamification vs clarityRiddle hints that give the answer away
The meatball silhouette made the reveal predictable, so the puzzle gave people little reason to engage beyond the prizes.
Digital tone vs productA clean interface for a hot, messy sandwich
The cold, clinical look sits awkwardly against an indulgent product, and weakens the "better for you" message.

Where the group took it next

The sections below were written by my teammates, building on the audit. I include them so the project reads as a whole.

Keep

Short-form video and ingredient-led visuals.

Remove

Content with no information and no way to interact.

Redesign

Calls to action, which were missing from the reel examined.

Risk worth taking

Raw, uncut preparation footage to show transparency.

Ethical issue

"Fresh" imagery with no nutrition information, while commenters expect a healthy choice.

Implication

The gap erodes the "better for you" positioning and trust over time.

Conceptual redesign

Keep the creative, drop the prizes, and ask people to tag the Sandwich Artist or branch they rate. Budgeted at AED 14,000 on paper. Never tested or implemented.

What this shows

Skills used: social media analysis, content auditing, evidence gathering from live posts, and structured written argument.

Working on this project helped me understand the importance of looking beyond individual social media posts and evaluating how a campaign works as a whole. By examining the campaign's objectives, context, and platform mix, I learned to consider whether the content, messaging, and choice of channels work together to support the intended goal. It also showed me that an effective social media strategy requires more than visibility and engagement; it needs a clear purpose and consistency between what a brand communicates and how it presents itself.
